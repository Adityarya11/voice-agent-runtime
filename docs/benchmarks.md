## How each number is measured

The driver (`scripts/bench/bench_latency.py`) opens a gRPC stream to the orchestrator gateway on `:50052`, the same interface AetherRTC uses, and sends 8 kHz 16-bit PCM in 20 ms frames paced in real time. Both services are started by the script with logs captured.

- **End of speech**: the instant the last speech frame is handed to gRPC. Each recording is trimmed so it ends within 20 ms of the last sample above about -40 dBFS, so this is the real end of speech and not the synthesiser's trailing silence. 300 ms of silence precedes each utterance and silence frames continue after it until the first reply byte arrives.
- **Client first audio**: the instant the first reply `AudioChunk` is received, minus end of speech. This includes the 800 ms of silence the VAD needs to close the utterance.
- **Server timers** come from one `BENCH` log line per utterance added to `_run_utterance` in `services/inference-py/main.py` (`time.perf_counter`, starting when the worker begins the utterance). STT is the whole `transcribe()` call including its temp-file write. TTFT is read from the existing `TTFT:` log line. TTS time wraps only `synthesize()`.
- **Mic gate** is emulated: sending stops when the reply starts, as AetherRTC drops mic frames while the agent speaks, and resumes after the reply has finished arriving.

### False triggers on synthetic non-speech

5 s of each stimulus, 10 seeds, count of trials where speech start fired. White noise at three levels, clicks, 1 kHz tone bursts, 4 Hz modulated noise, 120 Hz hum. This is a weak negative control: no keyboard, television, other voices or room noise.

| Setting | white_noise_500 | white_noise_2000 | white_noise_6000 | clicks_every_400ms | tone_bursts_1khz | modulated_noise_4hz | hum_120hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| max_dip_frames_0 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 |
| max_dip_frames_2 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 |
| max_dip_frames_3 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/10 |

Reading: speech must score above the 0.5 threshold on 8 consecutive frames (about 250 ms) before an utterance starts, and one sub-threshold frame in that window resets the count (`services/inference-py/vad/detector.py`, `_handle_speech_starting`). A one-word utterance is about 15 frames long, so a hesitation of one or two frames in the first part of the word can leave the longest run one frame short. Resetting the detector at the utterance boundary does not remove the misses, so stale recurrent state is not the cause. Detection of the same audio varies with how much silence precedes it, which points at the model's response to the word onset after silence together with the strict consecutive-frame rule. Tolerating short dips recovers only part of the misses. Not adopted in `main.py`: tuning on a single 0.48 s utterance would be overfitting, and a proper fix needs more short utterances and real-noise negatives. Synthetic single-voice speech; real callers will differ.

## What this does not measure

- Browser capture, WebRTC jitter buffers and playout. The client here is a gRPC client of the orchestrator, so AetherRTC's media path is not in the end-to-end numbers. Its two fixes are measured separately above.
- Real speech. Utterances are Piper-synthesised, one voice, passed through G.711 8 kHz. Whisper and VAD will find this easier than a noisy phone line, so STT time and WER are optimistic.
- Digital silence between utterances; no background noise, so VAD false positives are not exercised.
- Playback time. The next turn starts about 1 s after the previous response finished arriving, not after it would have finished playing.
- A cross-process clock. The residual in the derived table absorbs everything between the VAD boundary and the client, and cannot be split further without synchronised clocks.
- Concurrency. One call at a time; there is no multi-session load test.
- A true first boot: the OS file cache is warm in the cold run.
- p99 with small n. A p99 over about 60 turns is within one or two samples of the maximum.
